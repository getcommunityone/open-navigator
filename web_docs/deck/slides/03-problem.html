The problem

91,438 local governments. No common record.

Scattered

Each county, city, township, school district and special district publishes agendas, minutes, video and budgets its own way: PDFs, vendor portals, video channels.

Slow to read

One meeting’s minutes can run dozens of pages. Following one issue across a state by hand is not realistic for a resident or a small nonprofit.

Easy to miss

By the time most people hear about a decision, the vote is over and the window to speak has closed.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, Government Organization Summary Report: 2022 & 2025 (91,438 local governments in 2025) · 3

The U.S. Census Bureau counted 91,438 local governments in 2025: counties, cities, townships, school districts and special districts. Each one publishes its records its own way. Some post PDFs, some use vendor portals, some only post video. There is no common format and no single place to look. Reading one city's minutes takes real time; following an issue across a whole state by hand isn't realistic for a resident or a small nonprofit. So most people find out about a decision after the vote, when the chance to speak has passed.
