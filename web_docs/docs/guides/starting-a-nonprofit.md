# Starting a nonprofit

Issue: [#1078](https://github.com/getcommunityone/open-navigator/issues/1078). First drafted 2026-11-26 from the
experience of CommunityOne Navigator's founder, who started this nonprofit in Tuscaloosa, Alabama.

Starting a nonprofit turned out to be harder and more expensive than expected, and good, plain guidance was hard to
find. This guide walks through the steps in the order they're done, with:

- **what each step is**, and who you file with;
- **the fee and the official page it comes from**. A fee we haven't confirmed on the official page says
  *to verify*; nothing is estimated;
- **what we learned**, including the mistakes.

It's written for Alabama first. Other states follow as their official pages are checked. **This is not legal or tax
advice.** It's one founder's path and the official sources behind it.

## The steps, in order

### 1. Choose a name and form the corporation with the state

In Alabama, a nonprofit becomes a legal entity by filing with the **Secretary of State**:

1. **Reserve the name.** A Certificate of Name Reservation is required before you file. You request it online through
   Alabama Interactive ("Name Reservation for Domestic Entities"). Fee: *to verify*. A nonprofit's name doesn't need
   "Corporation" or "Inc."
2. **File the Domestic Nonprofit Corporation Certificate of Formation** (often called the articles of incorporation).
   File the original and one copy. Fee: **$200.00** to the Secretary of State.

Source: Alabama Secretary of State, [Domestic Corporations](https://www.sos.alabama.gov/business-entities/domestic-corporations)
(checked 2026-11-26).

**What we learned**

- **Do this first.** The IRS says to form the corporation with the state before applying for an EIN, and the bank and
  the IRS ask for the state record.
- **You need a board before you file.** The certificate names the initial directors, so pick your board first. We had
  to stop and choose ours partway through.
- **Get the certificate right the first time.** We made mistakes on ours, and fixing them means filing again. Before you
  submit, check:
  - the exact name, matching the reservation;
  - the registered agent and address;
  - the directors;
  - the nonprofit purpose;
  - the clause saying what happens to assets if the organization dissolves. The IRS looks for this when you apply for
    tax exemption.

### 2. Get an EIN from the IRS

The Employer Identification Number is the organization's federal tax ID. Apply online. **It's free**: "You never have
to pay a fee for an EIN." The online application gives you the number in minutes during its open hours.

Source: IRS, [Apply for an EIN online](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online)
(checked 2026-11-26). Beware of paid sites that look official; the IRS application is the only one you need.

**What we learned:** we were asked for proof of address along the way. Keep a document showing the organization's
address ready.

### 3. Open a bank account

Banks ask for the state formation record and the EIN, so this comes after steps 1 and 2. Keep the organization's money
separate from your own from day one; the IRS application in step 4 asks about it.

### 4. Apply to the IRS for tax-exempt status (501(c)(3))

Being a corporation doesn't make you tax-exempt. You apply to the IRS with **Form 1023**, or the shorter **Form
1023-EZ** if you qualify. Fees and eligibility: *to verify* on the IRS's current Form 1023 pages.

CommunityOne Navigator's exemption was issued in May 2026 (public record: ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, EIN
42-2467803). Once granted, donations to the organization are tax-deductible.

### 5. Write your bylaws and first board policies

Bylaws are your internal rules: how the board is chosen, meetings, officers, voting and conflicts of interest. They
aren't filed with the state, but the IRS application asks about them, and a bank or funder may ask for a copy. A
conflict-of-interest policy is worth adopting at the same time.

*Our status: still to write.*

### 6. Keep it in good standing every year

- **File a Form 990 return every year.** Small organizations, with gross receipts "normally $50,000 or less", file the
  **990-N (e-Postcard)** online, due by the 15th day of the 5th month after the tax year ends. **Missing three years
  in a row automatically revokes your exemption.** Source: IRS,
  [990-N e-Postcard](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/annual-electronic-filing-requirement-for-small-exempt-organizations-form-990-n-e-postcard).
  Larger organizations file the 990-EZ or 990 (thresholds: *to verify* on irs.gov).
- **State filings.** Check the items below.

## Easy to miss: check whether these apply to you

These steps we haven't done or confirmed yet. Each points to who to ask:

| Item | Why it matters | Who | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charitable solicitation registration | Many states require charities that ask the public for donations to register before soliciting | Alabama Attorney General (charities) | *to verify* |
| State sales tax exemption | Being 501(c)(3) doesn't automatically exempt purchases from Alabama sales tax | Alabama Department of Revenue, Sales & Use ("Exempt Entities") | *to verify* |
| Business privilege tax | Applies to entities organized in Alabama; check whether and how it applies to a nonprofit | Alabama Department of Revenue | *to verify* |
| City or county business license | Some cities require one even for nonprofits | Your city and county | not done |
| Insurance (general liability, directors and officers) | Protects the board and volunteers; often required by venues and funders | An insurance agent | not done |
| An attorney's review of the certificate and bylaws | Catches mistakes like the ones we made in step 1 | A nonprofit attorney or a legal-aid clinic | not done |

## What it cost us

So far, the filings with confirmed fees are the **$200** Secretary of State filing fee and a **free** EIN. The name
reservation and IRS application fees will be added once confirmed on the official pages. Lawyer, insurance and local
license costs: none yet. If you hire a formation service, compare its price against the state and IRS fees above.
